📚 Theoretical Framework

The Energy Explorers website uses educational theories to guide the design of learning activities. These theories help learners develop scientific knowledge, collaborate with others, connect learning to real-life experiences and use technology meaningfully.

💬

1. Community of Inquiry (CoI)

The Community of Inquiry framework focuses on teaching presence, social presence and cognitive presence.

  • Teaching presence:Lesson notes and teacher guidance.
  • Social presence:Padlet discussions and sharing community experiences.
  • Cognitive presence:Investigations, quizzes and problem-solving activities.
🧠

2. Constructivism

Constructivism explains that learners build new knowledge by connecting it to what they already know.

For example, learners use their existing knowledge of sunlight, torches, batteries and cooking to understand energy forms and sources. Activities encourage learners to explain concepts in their own words.

🏡

3. Situated Learning

Situated learning connects classroom knowledge to real-life situations and the community.

The Community Energy Investigation asks learners to explore how energy is used in their homes, schools and communities. This makes learning meaningful and relevant to their lives.

🪜

4. Scaffolding

Scaffolding provides support as learners develop new skills and knowledge. The support is gradually reduced as learners become more confident.

Learners first study illustrated notes, then complete matching games, answer quiz questions, investigate energy use and reflect independently.

💻

5. TPACK

Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge (TPACK) explains how teachers combine subject knowledge, teaching methods and technology.

On this website, the teacher combines knowledge of energy with suitable teaching approaches and digital tools such as Wordwall, Padlet, videos and online quizzes.

🔎 Activity 5: Energy Detectives – Investigating Energy

Subject: Natural Sciences and Technology

Grade: 4

Theme: Energy and Energy Transfer

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this activity, learners should be able to:

  • Identify examples of energy use at home and school.
  • Identify ways to save energy.
  • Collect information through observation and interviews.
  • Suggest solutions to energy-related problems in their community.

👀 Step 1: Observe

Look around your classroom or think about your home. Identify three things that use energy.

Examples: lights, televisions, radios, sunlight and cooking appliances.

📝 Step 2: Investigate

Complete the table below in your workbook.

Object Energy source How it is used
Classroom light Electricity Provides light
Laundry drying outside Sun Provides heat for drying
Radio Battery Produces sound

👨‍👩‍👧 Step 3: Ask Your Family

Ask a parent, grandparent or another trusted adult:

  1. How did people in our community obtain light in the past?
  2. How did people cook food before modern appliances were available?
  3. What can our family or school do to save energy?

💡 Step 4: Solve a Problem

Identify one way your school or household wastes energy. Suggest two ways to reduce the waste.

🎨 Step 5: Share Your Findings

Create a small poster titled“Save Energy in Our Community”.

Your poster should include:

  • Three energy-saving tips.
  • One example of knowledge you learned from your family or community.

🌟 Energy Detective Mission:Observe, investigate, ask questions and help your community save energy!
